The Ultimate Guide to RWA Token Standards: ERC-20, ERC-1400, ERC-3525, and ERC-7943 Explained [2026]

Every tokenized real-world asset is built on a token standard. Get the standard wrong and your token cannot list on major exchanges or interact with DeFi. This ultimate guide explains ERC-20, ERC-1400, ERC-3525, and the 2026 industry-converged standard ERC-7943 — and what each means for investors.

📅 2026-08-21⏱ 9 min read🏷 Education · Infrastructure

Every tokenized real-world asset is built on a token standard. The standard is the blueprint — a set of rules that defines how the token behaves: how it can be transferred, who can hold it, what compliance checks run automatically, and whether it can interact with DeFi protocols. Choose the wrong standard and you cannot list on major exchanges. Build on a non-compliant standard and regulators may not recognize your token as a valid security.

This is the complete guide to RWA token standards in 2026 — from the foundational ERC-20 through the compliance-focused ERC-1400, the semi-fungible ERC-3525, and the industry-converged neutral standard ERC-7943.

Why Token Standards Matter for RWA

In traditional finance, a stock is a stock — the legal framework governing ownership and transfer is the same regardless of which broker you use. In tokenized finance, the standard on which a token is built determines everything: which wallets can hold it, which exchanges can list it, which DeFi protocols can accept it as collateral, and which compliance checks run automatically on every transfer.

The wrong standard creates real problems. A tokenized bond built on a standard that does not support transfer restrictions cannot enforce KYC/AML requirements automatically — meaning every transfer has to be manually reviewed, destroying the efficiency advantage of tokenization. A standard that does not support partitions cannot represent different classes of the same asset in a single smart contract — requiring separate contracts for each tranche, each of which must be deployed, audited, and maintained separately.

ERC-20: The Foundation — and Its Limitations for RWA

ERC-20 is the base token standard on Ethereum, established in 2015. It defines six core functions: total supply, balance of an address, transfer, transferFrom, approve, and allowance. Every DeFi protocol, every DEX, every crypto wallet supports ERC-20 tokens natively.

For many RWA use cases, ERC-20 alone is insufficient:

  • No native transfer restrictions: ERC-20 tokens transfer freely between any addresses. Securities law in most jurisdictions requires transfer restrictions — accredited investor verification, geographic limits, lock-up periods. ERC-20 cannot enforce these natively.
  • No built-in compliance hooks: Regulators expect issuers to maintain a compliant investor registry. Pure ERC-20 has no mechanism for this.
  • No partition support: A fund with senior and junior tranches needs to represent both classes — ERC-20 cannot distinguish between them in a single contract.

ERC-20 remains the standard for tokens where free transferability is the goal — stablecoins, governance tokens, utility tokens. For regulated securities, it is the foundation that compliance-focused standards build on top of, not the standard itself.

ERC-1400: The Security Token Standard

ERC-1400 was designed specifically to address ERC-20's limitations for regulated securities. It adds several critical capabilities:

Transfer restrictions by rule. ERC-1400 allows issuers to define transfer validation logic that runs automatically on every transfer attempt. A transfer from an unverified wallet fails. A transfer to a restricted jurisdiction fails. A transfer during a lock-up period fails. These checks happen at the smart contract level — no manual intervention required.

Forced transfers and recovery. For regulated securities, issuers sometimes need to force-transfer tokens (court orders, inheritance, regulatory action) or recover tokens from lost wallets. ERC-1400 includes these operator functions that pure ERC-20 does not.

Document attachment. Regulated securities must be associated with legal documentation — the prospectus, the subscription agreement, the investor rights document. ERC-1400 allows documents to be attached on-chain, creating a permanent link between the token and its legal basis.

Partitions. Different classes of the same security (common vs. preferred, senior vs. junior) can be represented within a single ERC-1400 contract using partitions, rather than requiring separate contract deployments.

ERC-1400 is widely used for tokenized equity, tokenized debt instruments, and fund shares that require regulatory compliance baked in. Its limitation: the compliance logic must be maintained by the issuer's operator infrastructure, which reintroduces centralization. The issuer can update transfer restriction rules — which creates governance questions about who controls the compliance layer.

ERC-3525: Semi-Fungible Tokens — the RWA Hidden Layer

ERC-3525 is the semi-fungible token standard — less well-known than ERC-20 or ERC-1400 but increasingly important for complex structured products.

The standard introduces a slot concept: tokens in the same slot are fungible with each other (like ERC-20 tokens of the same contract), but tokens in different slots are distinct (like NFTs). This creates a middle ground between fully fungible tokens and fully unique NFTs.

The RWA applications are significant:

  • Structured products: A collateralized loan obligation (CLO) has multiple tranches. Each tranche is fungible within itself but distinct from other tranches. ERC-3525 represents this naturally — different slots for AAA, AA, and B tranches.
  • Revenue-sharing tokens: Tokens that represent different revenue streams from the same underlying asset — a real estate property's rental income vs. its appreciation rights — can be separate slots in a single ERC-3525 contract.
  • Bond coupon separation: Individual coupon payments on a bond can be stripped and traded separately from the principal, as different slots — enabling bond stripping natively on-chain.

ERC-7943: The 2026 Neutral Standard — Why It Matters

ERC-7943 emerged as the industry's converged neutral standard for RWA tokens in 2026, according to Stobox's mid-year research report. This is the most significant recent development in RWA token infrastructure and the one most mainstream coverage missed entirely.

The problem ERC-7943 solves: the RWA market has multiple competing standards (ERC-20 for simple tokens, ERC-1400 for securities, ERC-3525 for semi-fungible, plus various proprietary extensions). This fragmentation means that a tokenized bond on one issuer's platform may not be compatible with a DeFi protocol built to accept a different compliance standard. Every integration requires custom engineering.

ERC-7943's approach is to define a minimal, neutral interface that any compliant RWA token can implement — regardless of the underlying standard it is built on. It provides:

  • Standard compliance metadata: A consistent way for wallets, exchanges, and DeFi protocols to query whether a token is compliant, what compliance checks apply, and what restrictions exist — without needing to know which underlying standard was used.
  • Cross-standard composability: DeFi protocols can implement ERC-7943 support once and accept any compliant RWA token regardless of whether it is built on ERC-1400, ERC-3525, or a proprietary framework.
  • Interoperability between issuers: Two issuers using different underlying standards can produce tokens that are mutually recognized by the same set of wallets and protocols — enabling secondary market liquidity across issuer platforms.

The analogy: USB-C is a standard interface that works regardless of what device is plugged in. ERC-7943 is the USB-C of RWA token standards — the layer that makes heterogeneous RWA tokens interoperable across the ecosystem.

RWA Token Standards — Quick Comparison
  • ERC-20: Foundation. Fully fungible. Free transfer. No native compliance. Used for stablecoins and utility tokens. Not sufficient alone for regulated securities.
  • ERC-1400: Security token standard. Transfer restrictions. Forced transfer. Document attachment. Partitions. Compliance logic maintained by issuer operator. Best for regulated equity, debt, fund shares.
  • ERC-3525: Semi-fungible. Slot-based partitions. Best for structured products, bond stripping, multi-tranche instruments where some positions are fungible and others are not.
  • ERC-7943: Neutral interoperability standard. Works on top of any underlying standard. Enables cross-standard composability. The ecosystem convergence layer for 2026 and beyond.

Which Standard Should You Care About as an Investor?

For most retail investors, the token standard is not something you will directly interact with. But it matters for two reasons:

First, it tells you something about the product's compliance architecture. A tokenized security built on ERC-1400 (or compliant with ERC-7943) has compliance baked into the contract — transfers cannot violate investor restrictions without the transaction failing. A security token built purely on ERC-20 is relying on external off-chain compliance monitoring, which is weaker.

Second, it determines composability. Tokens that are ERC-7943 compliant can interact with a broader ecosystem of DeFi protocols — potentially enabling their use as collateral, in yield strategies, or in liquidity pools. Tokens on proprietary standards that do not implement ERC-7943 are siloed within their issuer's own platform.
